# Pure Tank
- Best items: Link Line, Double-Layered Armor, Iron Tire

## Explanation
Pure tank itemisation should be aimed, as a general rule, at any hero that doesn't have any pressing mechanical reason why they should be hitting things hard. Any hero without innate effects to boost their AP or crit rate fall into this category. Heroes with effects that boost them when taking hits (like Frank, Merisi etc.) also fall in here. A purely vanilla hero with no in-fight effects falls into this category for the simple reason that if you're not doing anything special, your job is to eat the damage so the people who are doing special things get to keep doing them.

One last type of hero to throw this itemisation on are heroes who aren't vanilla, but who only need to exist in a fight to apply their effects. Examples of this include Peiniang Zhu and Big Foot - items don't really touch their effects (with some very limited examples), and they proc based on other people doing things. That means all you really want to do with them (again, aside from some specific examples) is to tank them up so they stick around to apply their effects more.

Pure tank itemisation should prioritise two stats above all else - Armor and Presence. Health is very important for tanks, certainly - but you'll usually get a good amount of that from levels and gold anyway, and a lot of tank items wind up with health on them as a matter of course. Armour, on the other hand, is a lot harder to come by and makes a huge difference in reducing incoming damage to keep your tank around a lot longer. Presence is just as vital, though, because a tank that isn't getting hit is a tank doing nothing.

Avoid pure health items like Big Guy Vest in almost all circumstances. Even on heroes with innate health scaling like Wukong and Frank, you're going to be better served grabbing armour to keep them around for longer because that health item is only going to be contributing a relatively small amount to their actual health total. Also generally avoid healing items on tanks (yes, Mandrake Elixir isn't great); they look good on paper, but in practice they're going to be eating a lot more damage than they could ever heal, such that an armour item will keep them alive and kicking for far longer. The only exception is if you have a hero with extremely high armour through other means (e.g. the Wolfgang variant that can self-stack armour).


## Example Builds
Link Line, Double-Layered Armor, "Mockery" Textbook
Double-Layered Armor, Glorious Shield, Heart Mirror

# Tank (one damage item)
- Best items: Self-Increasing Dumbbell, Gravity Converter, Giant Warhammer

## Explanation
These guys are very similar to Pure Tanks. The distinction between the two is that this latter category has some kind of innate mechanic to make them something of a threat - either something to boost their AP, or something to give them extra attacks.

While these heroes have something extra in their kit to make them more dangerous than a dumb brick wall, in most cases their innate mechanics aren't enough in isolation to make them any more than a dumb brick wall with a few spikes on top. For these guys you'll want to consider a standard tank build, but throwing in a single item somewhere to help them really scare the things they're hitting. Your rule of thumb should be that if you're a tank getting extra attacks (i.e. if you're Merisi) you should look for something which procs on-hit; if you're not, take something that boosts your one attack more based on your bulk.

Importantly, this category does not fit tanks that have a single-use effect. Heroes like Hakuna and Reinhardt might have some nice team-wide AoE on turn one, but after that they become vanilla (okay Reinhardt has some minor splash, sometimes, but the damage is so low as to be irrelevant). It isn't a good idea to base your item build around a single attack unless that attack is the be-all and end-all of the entire hero.

It is critical, however, to only apply this category when there's a relevant item to slot in. You can't just throw any old damage item in there and expect it to have any real relevance; a Hundred Atk Sword or Sword of Heaven isn't going to do jack. Slot in a damage item if there's an item which synergises with that hero's bonus stats or innate effects; if there isn't itemise them like a Pure Tank.

Note that Merisi, as the only real tank to get bonus attacks, is a great user of Acceleration Coil since he'll stick around long enough to stack it and his AP is low enough that pretty quickly it'll overtake his actual attacks for damage. Helium Tank is a strictly worse alternative, being more expensive and quickly being outscaled in exchange for ignoring armour. Fearless Headband, on the other hand, is pretty fantastic.

N.B. While itemisation timings vary, you'll usually want your damage item to be your second item, sometimes third. Getting an actual tank item in place is almost always necessary to let them actually function (a Merisi without a Presence item is a Merisi who never gets an extra attack). Once that's in place, THEN you can afford to get them a damage item - and you'll usually want this place second so they can start really impacting fights. Only push it to third item on heroes like Frank, for whom dealing damage is very much a luxury.

## Example Builds
- Link Line, Gravity Converter, "Mockery" Textbook
- Glorious Shield, Self-Increasing Dumbbell, Double-Layered Armor

# One Slap Chap
- Best items: Armor-Piercing Round, Lonely Penguin, Ghost Claw, Terror Anthology

## Explanation
A one-slap chap is a hero who shows up to a fight, does one special attack to the entire team, then lapses into being vanilla. Importantly, Lubos is not a one-slap chap because he pokes rather than slaps. Or possibly because he actually deals damage. Take your pick.

Again these heroes have very similar itemisation to the Pure Tanks. The differentiation here is that their special attack targets the entire team and in the first turn - meaning they're a fantastic vector for any item-based debuffs that your current patch has access to. Armor-Piercing Round is the most ubiquitous of these, and has a drastic impact if you can get it applied immediately in fights, but other debuffs can also be relevant thanks to their early application.

Note that while I've listed it above, Lonely Penguin is not something to throw on without thinking about it. Only build it if at least one of your damage dealers is also building it - that way your tank can apply the debuff so your damage dealers guarantee their damage bonuses.

## Example Builds
- Link Line, Armor-Piercing Round, "Mockery" Textbook
- Glorious Shield, Lonely Penguin, Double-Layered Armor 

# Bruiser
- Best items: Charged Barrel, Mechanical Arm, Dragon Slayer, Golden Ticket

## Explanation
At last moving away from the Pure Tank itemisation categories and into something with some weight being it, the Bruiser is a catch-all term for anything which is bulky but expected to be a durable damage-dealer rather than a bullet sponge with a fist attached. Notably the game separates this into the Fighter and Gladiator subcategories, but this only really influences base stat growth.

Usually you'll be looking for items here which boost both damage and survivability to some extent or other, or which provide a lot of flat stats without needing to synergise with others. You should avoid items which place much emphasis on crit (though don't disregard crit items entirely, some like Bunny Dagger may be worthy of consideration in a weaker item version).

IMPORTANT - Plate of Agony would be a great item for this kind of hero, but currently seems to be bugged - so far as I can tell, it functions exactly how it's supposed to except its stacks never clear. This means that in longer fights, rather than delaying incoming damage, it instead stacks up huge amounts and unavoidably kills you.
This seems to have been patched at this point, but still use at your own risk.

## Example Builds
- Chicken Rib, Dragon Slayer, Gravity Converter
- Height Detector, Golden Ticket, Mechanical Arm

# Assassin
- Best items: Little Lightning, Conversion Gear, Obedient Whip, Sharp Piercer

## Explanation
Quite the contrast to the previous archetypes, Assassin itemisation is all damage, all the time. Disregard any downsides, you don't care how squishy you get - that ADC can't hurt you if he's dead before he gets to shoot, after all.

Notably, there are two kinds of items that can reasonably fall into the Assassin category. The first is the more obvious Assassin item - things with a whole lot of damage and no defences, or sometimes even negative defences. The second kind, on the other hand, is less obvious - offensive items with smaller bonuses whose main benefit is they're cheap. Assassins like to snowball off kills, and one way to do that is to make your items cheap enough to chain together, letting you turn one kill into many more.

Much like with bruisers, you should usually avoid crit items for your Assassins - you want to reliably murder squishies, not do it if you land your 40% crit. The obvious exception to this rule is when your hero's mechanics revolve around crits such as with Manta or Lan - for the former you want enough crit to hit 100% without going over, and for the latter you want as much as you can get.

Note that as with ADCs, there's an argument to be had for picking up a GA effect like Phoenix Feather or Dissonance on your Assassins. In this case, though, it's usually not worth it - Assassins are usually melee and you expect them to fall over in teamfights. You just need to build to make sure they deal crippling damage before they do.

## Example Builds
- Little Lightning, Sharp Piercer, Carnivore's Medical Record
- Handy Knife, Isabelle, Obedient Whip

# Multi-Hitter
- Best items: Acceleration Coil, Helium Tank, Ghost Claw, Terror Anthology

## Explanation
These guys are a little weird. They don't necessarily hit hard, but they do hit often, and that means a whole different approach to itemisation - look for items which give you damage or stacking debuffs from attacks.

This category has a lot of flexibility in it because there are plenty of heroes that attack more than once but don't really go overboard - Elemi, for instance, along with Kid, Reinhardt and others. These heroes can be built as multi-hitters but won't excel in the role - you'll usually want to build them via one of the other categories unless they're the only multi-attacker in the match, in which case you can lean into that aspect a little more.

Multi-hitters also don't really need to be classed as such to do their job; you can build them via other categories and they'll perform just fine. This only really becomes a role in itself when there's multiple strong, appropriate items available in your version. Nonetheless, you should keep it in mind when building one of the relevant heroes. You should also bear in mind that these builds can fall flat if you're doing badly in a game for the simple reason that they rely on a lot of instances of damage; if fights are shorter and more lethal they won't have time to do their work.

## Example Builds
- Acceleration Coil, Helium Tank, Terror Anthology
- Fearless Headband, Ghost Claw, Extendable Sword

# AoE
- Best items: Blue Monolith, Stealth Dagger, Dissonance

## Explanation
The AoE category struggles a little to differentiate itself, and instead slots somewhere in between the Assassin and Multi-Hitter archetypes. While not very similar in mechanical terms to Assassins, your approach is similar - deal as much damage before you die as possible; mechanically they're much closer to Multi-Hitters, the main difference being that multi-hitters tend to have lower damage on each target, while AoE heroes deal full damage to everything (but usually with a drawback of some kind).

There aren't many true AoE heroes in the game - in fact, that list above is arguably all of them - and they basically all use magic items. Look to maximise your damage potential and don't worry too much about anything else - damage, damage and more damage. Debuffs and such are nice, but they can be applied by heroes with less power behind them. Your AoE-users are there to throw the biggest, most potent splashes of damage possible before they inevitably get shanked.

## Example Builds
- Blue Monolith, Locust Swarm, Archmage Hat
- Psychic Glove, Stealth Dagger, Locust Swarm

# ADC
- Best items: Sword of Heaven, Handy Knife, Laser Sword, Boxing Gloves

## Explanation
You'll have an ADC in the vast majority of comps you play, and for good reason - you'll usually need someone whose job it is to deliver single big punches in teamfights. ADCs have very low defensive stats and there's not really any getting around that; don't worry about defensive items except maybe a GA effect (or Stealth Dagger on the ADCs that have access to it).

The big difference between ADC and Assassin itemisation is you'll care a lot less about budget for ADCs and a lot more about crit. ADCs have naturally high AP which works very nicely with crit; you'll almost always want to be aiming for as close to 100% as you can reasonably get. The only time you won't want to build crit items on your ADC is if they're slotting into another itemisation archetype (like Felyn) or there just aren't enough crit items in your version to make a build out of.

Don't worry too much about item costs for your ADC - their entire reason to exist is for insurance for late game teamfights. Don't get silly with it of course - BaJie is the only one who can pick the Ultimate Iron Fist without being comically optimistic - but expect your build cost to break 9k gold. The exception to that rule is with first-build items, like stacking ones (Physics Textbook can be very nice) or Chicken Rib, which can help to bring your overall cost down to something a little less exorbitant.

## Example Builds
- Sword of Heaven, Handheld Telescope, Laser Sword
- Physics Textbook, Limitless Chip, Carnivore's Medical Record 

# Mage
- Best items: Fragile Sticker, Blue Monolith, Stealth Dagger, Jar of Doom

## Explanation
At first glance, this really isn't a category. After all, it's another 'all the damage' archetype - so what's the difference between a Mage and an Assassin, or an ADC?

The answer is in what you're allowed to build. Although there's fairly similar numbers of Physical and Magical heroes, the vast majority of the latter are either mages or supports - and supports don't tend to get enough gold to qualify for this archetype.

That means that Mage itemisation is similar to your Assassins or ADCs, but with access to a set of items that far fewer heroes can use - often your Mage is the only one that has access to these items and has the gold to buy them.

Mage items also have a much greater tendency to offer big percentage damage boosts - Blue Monolith and Jar of Doom being good examples - which work out to a lot more power than the flat AP you get on physical items. They also don't have much access to crit. That means you're just looking for damage-boosts and mage-specific utility above all else, with flat AP a welcome secondary concern.

## Example Builds
- Stealth Dagger, Blue Monolith, Jar of Doom
- Archmage Hat, Obedient Whip, Dissonance

# Buffer
- Best items: Lunchbox, Stimulant, Scallion Oil Noodles, Herd Immunity Flyer

## Explanation
Heading into the Support archetypes, the Buffer is a semi-rare category which specifically includes heroes which apply buffs to friendly units either team-wide or repeatedly (ideally both).

Although both relevant heroes and relevant items are quite few in number, this is nonetheless an important category to keep in mind because when you can combine them they produce disproportionately powerful effects. Paisai alone is a useful, if generic, support; Paisai with both Stimulant and Scallion Oil Noodles has a drastic effect on your team's stats.

Where there aren't enough buffer-specific items to fill out a build, you should still do as much as you can - buff-oriented items are very cheap and extremely potent - before moving into the Seat-Warmer category below to fill the rest of the item slots.

## Example Builds:
- Stimulant, Scallion Oil Noodles, Lunchbox
- Stimulant, Herd Immunity Flyer, Potion Pouch

# Seat Warmer
Best items: Battle Flag, Interest Note, Herd Immunity Flyer

## Explanation
This category belongs to the heroes who sit in the Support role - and therefore get very little gold - but who have few or no in-fight mechanics. They don't really do any damage with their attacks, AP has little to no relevance to their effects. You brought them for their cards or their passive effects.

In a perfect world you'd itemise these heroes as Pure Tanks so that they would actually provide an in-fight benefit - by soaking damage - but this world is far from perfect and your support doesn't have the money to buy those kinds of nice things. Instead you should look to items which have a decent impact but cost substantially less - allowing your support to give you some relevant item spikes sometime before Hell freezes over.

Note that aside from a few extremely powerful support-buff items (Herd Immunity Flyer, most notably), if the game goes super late and/or there are version rules which promote Support gold generation, these builds will become inferior to simple Pure Tank (or, in some cases, Mage) ones. The point is that in the nine games out of ten that that DOESN'T happen, you're better off having three mediocre items than two decent ones. All the same, consider throwing cheaper tank items into this category as well to add to soak efficacy (Double-Layered Armor is a great one since it costs about the same anyway).

## Example Builds
- Interest Note, Herd Immunity Flyer, Scallion Oil Noodles
- Battle Flag, Fragile Sticker, Double-Layered Armor 

# Special Case
- Best items: N/A

As with any categorisation system (at least one looking at a reasonably complex data set), there's always going to be a few outliers that just don't really fit in anywhere. Be ready to throw all these rules out the window for certain heroes, and to heavily modify them for others. I won't go into detail on these heroes - that's saved for their individual entries - but will give a brief summary of the most notable ones below:

- Peiniang Zhu: Not an AoE mage or multi-hitter but has a huge number of potential damage instances - effects which proc specifically off damage rather than attacks are amazing.
- Digo: Has absolutely no interest in defences of any kind, ever, no matter what.
- Zealot: Weirdness around clones means flat stats at the start of a fight are key.
- Babe: Massive discount on your first item. Either get something stacking very early or get that Ultimate Iron Fist for less than the price of a Little Lightning.
- Raven: Only hero that benefits from penetration more than just directly.
- Felicity: Eats debuffs. Don't use them.
