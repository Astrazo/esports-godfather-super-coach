## Hero Description
Hoo boy, this guy. There's an absurd number of interactions possible with Fernan, so many that they're getting their own section below... but I suspect he'll be the reason for half my post-3.1 views, so I'mma have to do him justice. Strap in folks, this'll be a long one.

To start with Fernan's cards are... weird. Weird because they mostly involve stealing other peoples' cards and using those instead. That's such a mixed bag that I'm just going to ignore it for now and we'll talk about it in that section. Instead, let's talk about fights.

In fights, Fernan has some awkward mechanics. When he hits an enemy hero he gains a stack of Reshape, giving himself 5 armour, 5% lifesteal and 8% bonus AP (the only hero in the game with innate lifesteal besides Nihil, incidentally). He then applies the Reference debuff to that target. Importantly, Reference does nothing whatsoever to the target; it's there essentially as a way to say 'Fernan already got his Reshape stack from this target'. If a target with Reference dies, however, Fernan gets 8% of their AP and removes all of the other Reference debuffs. The AP gain is permanent, though the game doesn't make that clear initially. It's also snapshotted at the moment you gain it - you don't get more AP when the target does.

This is a lot of moving parts to essentially just give you a fairly resilient bruiser. While he does gain AP from two different sources, in both cases the numbers are low enough that Fernan's never going to take over a game from them - they're just high enough to allow him to exist and not be ignored.

In isolation, Fernan's kit makes him a passable, resilient bruiser in a similar category to Nihil - never getting enough power to dumpster opponents, but existing as a hard-to-kill hero that still outputs passable damage. This needs to be taken in the context of his absurd cards, though, which can massively warp what he's able to do in fights. As a result, whether he's worth playing entirely depends on what interactions you can squeeze out of him; generally you'll want to sit him in top lane unless there are specific cards you're targeting.

His cards though... oh boy.

# Cards
## Reappearance
### Description
Pick a card from the hero you targeted [with Extract Memory]. Create a copy in hand belonging to Fernan and give it Single-Use and Vanish.

### Card Analysis
Generated through Extract Memory and nothing else. Costs nothing and will disappear when you end your turn, so make sure you play it unless you really don't want anything they're offering.

## Mirror Flash
### Description
Move to any spot on the field. The cost of this card then becomes equal to the number of hexes travelled [base cost 2].

### Card Analysis
Just as screwy in its own way as the rest of Fernan's kit, you can literally have him appear wherever the hell you want him to. Use caution though - if you go too far, you'll basically render this card unplayable for the rest of the game, so make sure you're okay with that. Importantly, if you've got a cost discount applied to this card, try to make sure you play it even if you don't want to move - if you play it and choose to go nowhere, you set its cost to 0 for when you do need it.

## Ephemeral Collection
### Description
Single use. Pick an enemy hero, then pick one of their cards; add a copy of that card to your hand. It's controlled by Fernan, has 3/4 tokens on it, and is removed when the last token is expended.

### Card Analysis
And this is where it starts. Importantly, despite the game stating 'a hero' in the card text, this is limited to enemies only. Pick a card, any card... and take it. Note that Ephemeral Collection (EC) is single-use, unlike Extract Memory (EM), and the generated card is not; that means you should avoid using it to grab single-use cards, since you'll still only get the one use from them. Instead, use EC to grab things you want to play several times (and for a more efficient SP price), while using EM to grab the singletons. Gets a lot less painful to play once you have the mastery to drop its cost to 2.

## Extract Memory
### Description
Target an enemy hero (global) and Attack. If you hit, spawn Reappearance in your hand linked to that hero.

### Card Analysis
The much worse version of Ephemeral Collection, this costs the same (with masteries) but needs you to land an attack and only generates single-use cards. Still allows some ridiculous things to be done. Note that the card generated doesn't vanish if unplayed (unlike Reappearance), so you can grab unplayable cards like Cache without penalty; the card does vanish if it leaves your hand though.

# Variants
None.

# Item Build
By default, you want Bruiser/Mage items on Fernan - he doesn't have big enough steroids or any multi-hits to really justify heavy AP gain, but he needs at least some damage to be an actual threat. Don't leave him too squishy since Reshape stacks are unreliable to generate.

# Funnelling
Probably not worth tutoring unless there's something really good you're looking for. Decent target for stats, though, since he's bulky enough to stick around to use them and has enough AP to deal decent damage.

Good - These heroes are a reason to pick Fernan.
OK - Not reasons to pick Fernan, but their cards will do if you need a target.
Bad - Their cards are terrible for Fernan, don't take them.

Good
Aurelio - Protagonist is great, and Inferno March will let you steal chunks of the enemy jungle while refunding most of the cost. Note that you won't be able to use Protagonist to tutor Flame Assault unless you use EC to create one first.
Peiniang Zhu - Since EM disregards token limits, you can play Thundercloud more than she can, and with solo lane levels. Kinda filthy.
Tiger Boy - Weirdly good, Whimsy will draw cards while refunding your investment with its discount. Double Trouble does, in fact, let you become a Tiger Boy with better stats.
BaJie - Pay 2 SP, generate a card that prints free money. Probably the best reason to keep a Patient Waiting in reserve.
Reinhardt - Cavalry Charge will let you start fights in bot lane from top lane (if the stars align). Supersonic is a decent utility tool, albeit at a huge SP premium.
Lubos - Duel is a fantastic way to murder solo tanks in top lane since it has no round limit on it. Knight's Oath also isn't terrible if your other lanes are getting ganked a lot.
Cubey - Great. You don't get his Evolution passive or anything, but you're still generating bonus stats. Little expensive, but worthwhile.
Hakuna - Dominant Onslaught lets you mess with mid from the comfort of your island. Note you'll get the Plunders after the fight (if you kill things), there's no need to spend 2 SP on generating them directly unless you've got a turret on 1 hp you really want knocked over.
Niels - Bound has some niche application, but the main reason this is in the Good category is you can generate Revelation to get the field power without having to, y'know, play Niels.
Babe - Gift Bundle is worth using to grab free cheap stats. Make sure you use EC to get it so it doesn't vanish, though, and remember you're capped at 25 stacks since you don't have his masteries.
Crank - Oblivion is too expensive for Crank to play for the sole reason that he can already apply his passive by attacking things. You can't. Steal it and play it right before an objective fight to get Crank's inevitability.
Paisai - Elixir and Barbarian Runes both have such good utility that they're worth playing even at a premium. Barbarian Runes in particular is a great EC target.
Lan - Since Step A/B/C all generate each other, you only have to EM one of them to become bootleg Lan - start with C for a head start. Save EC for Reboot since Step C is single-use anyway and generates a fresh Step A when played.
Ada - She has to complete an entire equipment to get a Mod Upgrade; you just need to hit an attack. On the other hand, she only pays 2 SP for it and you pay 6, so I guess that evens out. Remember Mod Upgrade is single-use, don't waste your EC.
Qin Hu - Okay, sure, Homefront is even more expensive for you than it is for Qin Hu. On the other hand, playing it makes you now do literally everything relevant that Qin Hu does. Until that Warden dies, you're a better version of Qin Hu than Qin Hu is.
Quinn - EC her Tea Time and get yourself ~50% crit by the end of the game. Yes. Very yes.
Foso - She only gets a single copy of Libra for a reason. You get as many as you want. Remember not to waste EC on it (though Astral Guardian isn't a terrible EC target).
Anna - Your choice between generating one of the three single-use cards immediately at a fair price, or generating Floral Language to get them later at a much better one. Either is solid.
Leon - Generate Eternal Brand (remember, single use, don't EC) and you can steal his effect - but bear in mind you don't auto-target Branded heroes because that's Leon's passive. You can grab Nightmare Slayer from EC if you want to murder your targets occasionally, but you're usually better off Branding tanks to give you some inevitability on them since you're not getting the focus targeting.
Felicity - Most of her cards are bad for you, but Return to Embrace can still net positive with the right setup. Blessing may be relevant in very niche scenarios.
Omaha - Traitor birb is here to help. You can steal both of Omaha's cards and get the benefit of the birb. Note that Wild Heart's stats are based on Fernan's, not Omaha's, so presumably it's actually Wild Heart's stunt double finally getting a moment in the spotlight.

Junglers
Tier list:

Key
S Tier: Heroes that can be safely blind-picked, with no real counters, strong cards and which counter some heroes; or heroes that have the potential to completely take over a game in the right comp

A Tier: Solid picks which have few counters, if any, and which can generally perform at least adequately in any given situation while having strong potential to excel, but lack some key tool(s) for being a complete package; or heroes that can become extremely powerful with the right comp and setup

B Tier: Heroes who are very situational, only working well with a great setup and/or against specific enemy heroes or comps (e.g. counterpicks); or heroes that can become extremely powerful, but are heavily countered by too many heroes to feasibly ban out for; or heroes that are good in fights but have terrible cards, or vice versa.

C Tier: Heroes that generally don't perform amazingly; they lack the tools to excel, meaning the best they can hope for is to perform their role in a satisfactory fashion.

D Tier: Heroes that will pretty much never do anything, really. This is the only tier of heroes you should generally avoid.

Explanations

S Tier: Kamaitachi is probably the single best general-purpose jungle Fighter, only let down by his lack of initiation. Zealot, meanwhile, can be utterly dominant if properly supported.

A Tier: Nihil and Reinhardt are solid frontline picks who don't need much in the way of assistance. Aurelio's kit is perfect for jungling and providing substantial teamfight damage, while Gillis will inevitably build up a big gold lead.

B Tier: Mostly situational picks. Hakuna is underwhelming but the best choice for a pure tank in the jungle. Mo varies between C and S tier depending on the current ruleset (i.e. whether he gets extra bounties or not).

C Tier: Bariel doesn't know what he wants to be; if you really want an ADC in the jungle, take Bond. Lubos, meanwhile, has situational appeal but ultimately doesn't bring enough to the team.

D Tier: Kaka sucks. 'Nuff said.

Drafting for Jungle

If I'm on blue side (and so single first pick) I'll usually draft a jungler with a plan in mind; if I'm planning on supporting my jungle I'll go for S tier, and if not, usually A tier unless I've got a specific plan in mind or I'm feeling spicy. Jungle bans usually aren't necessary unless you need to ban Gillis to avoid a counterpick.
