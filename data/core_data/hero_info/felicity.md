## Hero Description

So, Felicity. Her existence in fights revolves around the Happiness debuff (misspelled as Happniess in v3.8 to my amusement), a debuff which she targets at the start of fights, deals damage equal to 15/20% of the target's AP after they take an action, and stacks whenever they deal damage. For clarity on exactly how the debuff works, see the entry further down. Whenever she attacks in fights, meanwhile, she cleanses all debuffs from her target - which is to her benefit, because Happiness can't be cleansed and instead deals damage when you try.

While this is her only contribution to fights, it's a big one. Even with no other mechanics kicking in, the ability to target a dangerous squishy and make them destroy themselves - not even that slowly since the damage is based on their own power - is incredibly powerful. Then you start getting into the underlying mechanics and how it stacks based on damage, not attacks, and you'll realise that we finally have an actual counter to the various multi-hitters that have been dominant in the game since release. Not that that stops them being dominant - the AI bans her all the time for a reason - but at least it's something.

On the field, meanwhile, Felicity has an absurdly powerful card combo to set up a huge, long-lasting teamfight - 8 round 4v4s on her first turn are not uncommon. This is limited-use due to Return to Embrace being token-based, but allows for some massive early game plays; it also provides much-needed support for teamfight comps fighting against skirmishers in allowing you to get a real fight where your comp can work as designed.

While she can be slotted into any role but ADC, the best places to run her are jungle or support. In the jungle she has the best access to big teamfight initiations, as well as using her unusually well-costed mobility to find opportunistic kills. In support, meanwhile, she still provides the debuff you brought her for, and since it's based on the target's AP rather than her own she doesn't suffer too much from a lack of gold. You can also bring her in a solo lane, but her initiation is terrible for 1v1s and she'll find it much harder to find good spots to fight in.

A word of caution however: while Felicity is a very strong hero with a lot going for her, she does not play well with others (ironically). The fact that she eats her own team's debuffs means she's almost as hard a counter to your own team as Wukong is, if you're not careful with your draft; she also dislikes friendly shields since her debuff only stacks if health damage is dealt. She won't fit into every comp, so think of her as something you either first pick or grab late in the draft as a counterpick.

N.B. While Felicity is marked as a melee, has access to melee items and melee stat growth, she has a ranged hero's Presence. It's unclear whether this is a bug or an undocumented feature.

# Cards
## Wriggle
### Description
Move one hex, then search for another of Felicity's hero cards and add it to your hand.

### Card Analysis
Step A. Rejoice, for in a rare show of reasonable thinking the developers decided to give you a cheap movement card. If anything this is undercosted - searching one of your cards is greatly necessary to facilitate your combo. Note that you have to move, there is no same-hex option (hexophobes!) so make sure you're going where you want to go when you play this (and keep it in mind if for some reason you want to search this to play it again instead of just searching the other combo piece).

## Return to Embrace
### Description
Every hero (friend or foe) within 2 hexes of Felicity moves to her hex. You gain 1 SP for each hero targeted. Starts with 1 charge and gains 1 every 20 rounds. Note that despite the text saying you can have two charges, the max is in fact 1.

### Card Analysis
Step B. Now you're in position from Wriggle, it's time to gain an absurd amount of SP while also pulling in every poor idiot who thought he was safe in lane, as well as their families, pets and passing wildlife. Note that the card text is wrong on two counts - not only do you only have 1 max charge (and so should dump it on cooldown to avoid waste), but your targets don't actually need to move to give you SP - even if they're already on your hex, you still get paid. Can be used as an SP refill even if you don't feel the need to follow it up, but that's probably a waste.

## Mirage Scales
### Description
Costs 2 SP plus one more for each hero in Felicity's hex. Initiate a fight with all heroes on Felicity's hex, with round count equal to the number of participants.

### Card Analysis
Step C. Payoff. Everyone's grouped up, so it's time to get a big ol' teamfight going. Assuming you're playing this off Return to Embrace (and that's usually the only time you'll play it), the fight is going to go on until one team is dead, so expect blood. Note that you could make this playable in lane with the cost reduction mastery (which would make it a cheaper Confrontation), but doing so means you miss out on a chunk of the debuff damage which is the entire reason you brought Felicity in the first place.

## Blessing
### Description
Target hero (friend or foe) has all buffs and debuffs removed. If you targeted an enemy they attack themselves once.

### Card Analysis
The black sheep of Felicity's card family, this doesn't interact with your other cards at all, instead just providing minor utility (or overpriced poke). You'll usually find yourself leaving this card unplayed, as its best application is to remove expensive or fight-turning buffs (e.g. I Am Justice, Traveler's Blessing) or indefinite duration debuffs (e.g. Death Finger). Note that in line with Felicity's theme, this card causes a self-attack and so can deal surprisingly good damage if targeting an enemy squishy. Further note: buff removal does not include shields. Targeting, for example, Traveler's Blessing, will result in the buff component being removed while the shield will remain (subject to the target's self-attack).

# Variants
None.

# Item Build
Mage/Support/Tank. Felicity can support a wide range of items. From the jungle you'll probably want to bring some AP items so she's got a chance of succeeding in ganks. In any role you'll want any good items which synergise with periodic damage ticks (i.e. Terror Anthology). For a support Felicity (or a jungle one if you have a high damage team) you'll probably want to lean into tank items thereafter since your debuff vanishes if she dies.

# Funnelling
Generally no. Her combo requires a token on Return to Embrace, which is going to limit you a lot more than finding her cards (especially since you have a built-in tutor). Her debuff is entirely agnostic to her own stats, and that's all her power, so don't bother.

# Interactions
Multi-hitters: Yep, you heard right, they have a counter now. Every instance of damage will add stacks, every action will cause damage. Kamaitachi quakes in fear.

Damaging items/debuffs: Again, it's every damage instance. Acceleration Coil, Lightning Mark, Frozen Stiff - any source of damage will stack up the debuff. Note that while these effects stack the debuff, it only triggers when the target takes an action or the debuff is cleansed.

Happiness and Attack mechanics:
Since Happiness is a little weird in how it operates, I've opted to put the full description here rather than in the main entry. I'll also list points regarding Felicity's cleanse mechanic. I'm listing the points as bullets, both to make them easier to read and (far more importantly) easier to write.

- Damage is dealt before the target actually takes their action, and can kill them before they act.
- Target gains stacks whenever damage is dealt. Damage absorbed by shields does not count as damage being dealt. Damage from debuffs does.
- Damage from the debuff is based on the target's current AP - very unusually for this game, the debuff damage updates dynamically as the target's AP changes.
- Debuff triggers from any action the target takes, even if the target doesn't deal damage with that action. This can even include pre-fight actions provided Felicity's debuff application is before them in the action order (e.g. Anna's first round armour is an action and will trigger Happiness).
- Debuff damage does count as Felicity's damage for the purposes of mechanics like Lifesteal.
- The target needs to actually take their action for the debuff to trigger. Stunned targets don't take actions and so won't trigger the debuff damage.

- Felicity cleanses debuffs on her target after she attacks them, but before any additional effects from that attack. This means her debuff damage will occur after her attack's damage, and means that if Felicity's attack bears any other debuffs (e.g. Tiger Boy's Double Trouble) they'll be applied without being cleansed.
- Only effects applied by the heroes themselves count as cleansable debuffs. Debuffs from items (e.g. Terror Anthology) will not be cleansed.
Mid Laners
Tier list:

Key
S Tier: Heroes that can be safely blind-picked, with no real counters, strong cards and which counter some heroes; or heroes that have the potential to completely take over a game in the right comp

A Tier: Solid picks which have few counters, if any, and which can generally perform at least adequately in any given situation while having strong potential to excel, but lack some key tool(s) for being a complete package; or heroes that can become extremely powerful with the right comp and setup

B Tier: Heroes who are very situational, only working well with a great setup and/or against specific enemy heroes or comps (e.g. counterpicks); or heroes that can become extremely powerful, but are heavily countered by too many heroes to feasibly ban out for; or heroes that are good in fights but have terrible cards, or vice versa.

C Tier: Heroes that generally don't perform amazingly; they lack the tools to excel, meaning the best they can hope for is to perform their role in a satisfactory fashion.

D Tier: Heroes that will pretty much never do anything, really. This is the only tier of heroes you should generally avoid.

Explanations

S Tier: Wolfgang murders whoever made the mistake of laning against him; Aurelio acts like a bruiser, a carry and a pseudo-jungler able to reach any lane from mid.

A Tier: All solid picks. I don't actually like Kamaitachi in mid at all, but can't deny his bully potential along with Qube. Bunu Shan does nothing in mid, but is still well placed there so you can run one of the super-high-attacking ADCs. Lan is a murderess, pure and simple. Enidi, Crank and Deadfire are all solid, reliable picks.

B Tier: Situational or comp-specific picks. Frank, Gillis, Kid, Hass, Digo and Nihil are all notable as heroes who perform very well in general, but are better suited to other lanes; nonetheless, viable mid lane picks, particularly if you want to swap them between lanes for counterpick potential.

C Tier: Niels and Elemi are both picks that beg your opponent to gank mid over and over; Cubey is quite vulnerable as well due to its self-damage. Justice simply can't bully lane like the better mid lane picks can and is somewhat vulnerable in mid. Miki's only job is to join objective fights he shouldn't be able to, and mid lane usually gets to join them.

D Tier: Tiger Boy, Fatty White and Palulu are all heroes that don't use solo lane gold well; Palulu in particular is terrible in mid because you can literally gank her with zero risk. Kaka, meanwhile, continues to be terrible.

Drafting for Mid Lane

I tend to leave mid as a late pick so that I can take whoever will fit my comp best; I'll only pick earlier if I'm going for a double-buff strategy and need Dylan. There's a raft of solid A-tier picks who can slot into most comps as needed, so there's not much risk in leaving picks until late; Wolfgang is the only concern, so you might need to spend a second round ban on him if the opponent plays him.
