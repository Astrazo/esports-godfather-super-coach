# Hero Summary
Support with a light card footprint who provides support for, and protection from, skirmish comps.

# Hero Analysis
## Attributes
### Hero Class
- Support
### Attack Type
- Ranged
### Damage Type
- Magical

## Hero Description
Anna has a lot of disparate effects between her passives and cards, but they're all fairly light touch. Her most ubiquitous trait is Fragrance - whenever your team enters a fight they gain Fragrance, which increases their armour by 40 for the first round of the fight (even if she's dead or absent). While 40 armour isn't exactly gamebreaking, it's certainly enough to make a big difference in damage output during that first round, and can really cause problems for teams that want to fight you constantly.

On top of this, Anna also provides Trial Before Awarding (activates whether she's there or not, but does not activate if she's dead). This allows you to pick a specific enemy hero to debuff (though the debuff is just a marker with no negative effects). If that hero dies during the fight, their killer is awarded with Rosemary, a buff which lasts for the rest of the game and gives that hero a 30/42 AP boost on their first attack of each fight.

Between these two effects, Anna is obviously built for frequent fights - if you're skirmishing you'll get steadily increasing benefits from securing kills while also being relatively resilient to the counterpunch. Unfortunately Rosemary tends not to be all that impactful, as it's low impact (30/42 AP isn't all that much really unless you've got a big multiplier behind it, plus it's only for the one attack) and also really quite hard to manipulate onto the hero you want getting it. Consider Anna a Fragrance vector, with Rosemary being a small added benefit, rather than the inverse.

Finally, Anna's cards are quite unique in that she actually only has one card that starts in the deck. When you play that one card you generate three more cards, all of which single-use, in your deck. This keeps your deck nice and lean, especially if you're picking her to ward off skirmishes and therefore don't want to use her cards much (which are largely skirmish-centric).

Between her light touch and her low impact but ubiquitous and positive effects, Anna comes in as a nice flexible defensive support you can drop into most comps without too many concerns. Her only real weakness (such as it is) is being drafted against teamfight comps, as her effects all wear off quickly and have no real benefit in longer, larger engages.

# Cards
## Floral Language
### Description
Spawn one copy each of Mandala Flower, Flame Orchid and Cosmos into your deck.

### Card Analysis
Quite expensive for not actually doing anything directly, by playing this card you're basically pre-paying SP for the high efficiency of the created cards. The cost reduction mastery helps to mitigate the delayed impact.

## Mandala Flower
### Description
Target an enemy hero (global); start a standard 3/1 fight centred on their hex.

### Card Analysis
A vanilla initiation card but without range restrictions and with a steep cost reduction. Most of the SP you paid for Floral Language is going into the cost reduction for this card. Nothing particularly stand-out about it otherwise aside from working on any lane.

## Flame Orchid
### Description
Target a friendly hero. If that hero has jungle creeps within 1 hex of them, they kill all of said creeps. If they don't, they gain the Flame Orchid effect, giving them 15 armour penetration on their first attack of their next fight.

### Card Analysis
The least useful of the three spawned cards, Flame Orchid is mostly useful for situational minor counterjungling; while this doesn't tend to matter all that much in the short term, the card is at least free (once created, that is). Try to avoid playing this with no creeps around, as the Flame Orchid effect is really quite unimpressive - like Rosemary it only works for one attack, but unlike Rosemary it only lasts for one fight. Can be situationally strong for a small number of heroes that hit very hard in the first round (e.g. Hakuna).

## Cosmos
### Description
Target a friendly hero and move them one hex.

### Card Analysis
Very simple and somewhat low impact, but happily does at least give you a movement effect that doesn't break the bank. Works nicely paired with Mandala Flower to give you a properly set up skirmish for very low relative SP cost, allowing you to fit in utility or pre-fight poke.

# Variants
None.

# Item Build
Support. Note that Fragrance counts as Anna taking an action and applying a buff, so any effects which proc off buffs (e.g. Scallion Oil Noodles) work nicely.

# Funnelling
No. She has no effects to make her worth stat-boosting, and tutoring her isn't really worthwhile.

# Interactions
Trial failed: Anna and Lubos are, per the small amount of lore we have, supposed to be linked (I think they're lovers or something? Honestly I forget). Unfortunately they don't actually work all that well together - Flame Orchid is often redundant given Lubos already has armour pen on his special attack, and importantly, Trial Before Awarding applies after Lubos attacks, making it very hard to actually get Rosemary onto him. Patch 3.1.14 was, I believe, supposed to have fixed this - but so far as I can tell, it only partially succeeded, if at all.

Sad birb: Raven's raison d'etre is to stack feathers to let him punch through armour. Fragrance provides ten feathers' worth of armour in the first round, leaving Raven unlikely to get his auto-crit and extremely vulnerable to being blown up before he can make his kit function.

Keep it tight: Anna's tiny deck footprint makes her the perfect support for a comp that wants to spam specific cards over and over. It's unfortunate that frequently the cards you want to spam are support cards (e.g. Beverly, Dylan), but nonetheless there are certain heroes who appreciate the light touch without really wanting to be tutored (Leon, Qube, Ada, Wukong are all examples).

Team Comp Approaches
As should hopefully have become apparent if you read through the earlier parts of this guide (or at least skimmed them - let's be honest, there's a lot to get through), there are a few approaches you'll generally want to take when drafting a comp for this game. Importantly these aren't fixed, but just general guidelines to how you'll plan for the early part of the draft - once you know what's banned out and a few picks have happened on either team you can adapt the rest of your strategy accordingly.

The approaches are:

Bot lane focus - Pick a dominant bot lane pair early and look to snowball them. This can either be done by picking a lane bully ADC and hammering the enemy with attacks, or by picking a strong scaling ADC and ganking repeatedly. Other lanes should provide frontline and gank support.

Examples:
Reinhardt / Kamaitachi / Wolfgang / Felyn / Blocker
Frank / Aurelio / Enidi / Gang / Paisai
Wukong / Gillis / Aurelio / Bajie / Acedia

Jungle focus - Similar to bot lane focus, but playing around a jungle-support pair. ADC should be a strong general purpose pick. This approach tends not to win as quickly since your dominant hero isn't pushing a lane, but is able to spread out and help every lane at various times.

Examples:
Kid / Zealot / Dylan / Elemi / Beverly
Qube / Kamaitachi / Bunu Shan / Digo / Beverly

Single hero - This approach basically says 'I want to play this hero' and then builds a comp around them. Exactly how you go about this varies depending on the hero you're working around, but often involves double supports.

Examples:
Cubey / Reinhardt / Aurelio / Digo / Blocker
Merisi / Kamaitachi / Dylan / Elemi / Beverly
Kid / Zealot / Lan / Gang / Lady Deadfire
Qube / Aurelio / Dylan / Bariel / Charon
Bart / Hakuna / Enidi / Niels / Palulu

Theme - This approach picks a specific aspect to work around such as skirmishing, debuffs or assassination. This usually requires very targeted bans.

Examples:
Big Foot / Hakuna / Crank / Bond / Peiniang Zhu (Death by debuff)
Qube / Zealot / Wolfgang / Ada / Paisai (Fight All Day)
Nihil / Mo / Xiangxi Ke / Bond / Lady Deadfire (Bye Bye Backline)
Kid / Mo / Gillis / Bajie / Acedia (Money Money Money)
v3.1 | Team Comp Approaches
A couple of new team comp approaches have cropped up as a result of v3.1's new hero pool. Note that as before, these are generalist approaches, not fixed comps - just themes around which to build a comp.

Alpha Strike

This comp looks to approach teamfights by delivering such a massive hammer-blow in the first round that everything after that is cleanup. You'll want to stack up the boat as much as possible, and optionally throw debuffs onto enemy squishies before big fights. Key unit is Chen Fengcheng. Note Reinhardt's armour shred happens after the boat hits.

Examples:

Chen Fengcheng / Hakuna / Enidi / Bond / Peiniang Zhu

Chen Fengcheng / Reinhardt / Lady Deadfire / Shougong Lei / Tiger Boy

Premeditated Murder

Here you look to combine targeted attacks with low-health-targeting assassins to pick and eliminate opponents one by one. Leon is basically required here, with Felicity a strong bonus.

Examples:

Justice / Leon / Xiangxi Ke / Bond / Felicity

Leon / Xiangxi Ke / Nihil / Foso / Crank
v3.1 | The Conqueror Series
New to v3.1 is the Conqueror Series game mode, a challenging roguelite mode which shifts gameplay dramatically. The major noteworthy changes:

- There is no training or athlete development
- Inherent Mastery trees and Mastery progression are disabled
- Training Points are awarded after each game (with a bonus at the end of each season), and spent on expanding hero pools, buying Skills (a replacement for Mastery progression), Match Traits and Athlete Cards
- Seasons last a variable number of games with updates periodically
- Successfully completing a season allows you to progress to the next difficulty, with gradually increasing penalties applied as a result of that difficulty

Importantly, note that TCS is not infinite - it will end after three seasons, assuming you don't lose before then. Each season you get one 'Insurance' which saves you if you lose (and, optionally, allows you to skip the last game of a season if unspent, since you'd be progressing either way).

Due to the new structure and the removal of a lot of mechanics you'll be so used to as to take for granted, TCS can be very challenging at higher difficulty levels. It certainly took me a while to un-learn the main game and get into the swing of TCS. But eventually...


Due to the roguelite nature of the mode it's impossible to give a straight up 'this is how you win' explanation; the bulk of this guide will help, but isn't necessarily fully relevant due to the absence of static Mastery trees. I have nonetheless endeavoured to provide a few hints and tips below to guide you to your TCS victory.

Good luck.
v3.1 | The Conqueror Series (cont.)

    Keep in mind that you can't guarantee any hero gets any specific Mastery points. This can really cause headaches for you early on if you're used to playing with a specific hero using their key Masteries. To give a basic example, a huge amount of Cubey's scaling comes from its Masteries giving it increased stat scaling per level... and now that's gone, at least until you find and buy it.

    Some heroes have more unique Mastery options in their trees than others. To compensate for this, the developers added a number of new Masteries for heroes that simply don't exist in the regular game mode. Additionally, where you've got a choice between two options, each option exists individually as a Skill - meaning you can have both. A good example of these changes can be found in Beverly - she's no longer stuck choosing between boosting her Renovation's AP or Health benefits and can instead boost both, and now has access to a unique Skill allowing her to play Maintenance on her Designated Target instead of a tower.
    (note - since there's no in-game list of the available skills I'm sadly unable to waste sixteen pages of this guide evaluating each one individually. I'm sure you're all very sad about that.)

    As well as the heroes' unique Masteries becoming skills, there's also a wide variety of more generic Skills on offer. These range from extremely unimpressive (great, gain a few AP when shielding allies on the field, I'm sure my ADC will make great use of that) to absolutely absurd. There is, for instance, a Skill which gives a hero flat 35% bonus crit; while this is tagged for Supports only, dear old Felicity is classed as a support. Thanks to the wide variety of Skills available you'll need to do your own analysis of which ones are good and which aren't (unless the developers add Skills to the archive at some point, in which case you can just wait for me to do it).

    While some Skills are admittedly pretty garbage, you should nonetheless focus on picking up something - anything - for the heroes you want to focus on as early as possible. Since there's no longer any flat health/AP gains from masteries, heroes instead gain health/AP per level from simply having any Skill equipped - and while they might look small, those bonuses build up. Purely anecdotally, it seems like getting the push in lane is heavily tied to this adjusted Mastery system as well since there's no longer any Athlete stats to modify it.

    For your early-run upgrades, focus on picking up enough skills to get 2-3 heroes for each role 2-3 Skills each. This should be enough to get you a good enough draft without going overboard burning all your TP. Alongside this, try to prioritise Tactics cards and Match Traits, as your starting selection is garbage and nothing respectively. Match Traits in particular are very cheap to purchase, and even a low impact one will do a hell of a lot more than nothing will.

    Bear in mind for your early games (your first season or so) that your deck will be full of the garbage Tactics cards the game starts you with. You'll need to replace them ASAP, but until then avoid any drafts that require you to frequently find and play specific cards. Yes, that means Felyn/Blocker is finally not an S-tier lane!

    While obviously you should still pick up power-picks when they're offered, try to avoid any emphasis on hero pool expansions for your first season or so. Skills are far more important initially - between the power of the Skills themselves and the bonus stats they're extremely impactful. You can pick up hero pool expansions later on - and happily, doing so in later seasons makes the chosen hero start with a few semi-random Skills. They may be garbage, but replacing them is cheap and buying them definitely isn't.

    When you do start expanding your hero pool, place an even higher priority on power picks than you already did. Anything which can be considered a very broad counter (e.g. Wukong, Hass, Bart) will soak a lot of bans, and that's a lot more important in this mode due to your more restricted hero pool options - doubly so in the higher difficulties when you start getting picks locked out. You should also pay attention to heroes the AI loves to ban (e.g. Felicity, Raven, Big Foot) because you can soak free bans on heroes you have no intention of ever playing.

    Speaking of the hero lock-out, once you're playing at high difficulty you'll need to be very conscious of it during every pick/ban. For clarity, this lock-out occurs whenever you pick a hero and causes you to be unable to play that hero until the lock-out clears - the AI can still pick them, but you can't. The lock-out clears at the mid-point and end of each season. This makes it essential that you don't burn all your best picks in the first draft of a lock-out, because by the third game of a six-game season you're going to be really struggling to put any kind of comp together. You'll also find yourself starved of bans since you'll need to be banning out heroes you'd otherwise early pick.

    Pay close attention to the Variants that show up, and in particular, the ones that shift heroes into roles that can carry heavily. In my pictured run above, for instance, Wolfgang shifted to Variant 2 early and I was able to abuse this to have him basically solo-carry several games. His mere existence allowed me to take a relatively weak draft, preserving my lock-out for other games.

    When it comes to your actual draft, you'll need to be extremely fluid. Early on you'll want to lean on a skirmish-heavy playstyle; your deck will be full of crap like Roam, so you'll want skirmish-reliant heroes to get any value out of them, while finding the cards to put more elaborate strategies together will be difficult. Later on you'll instead be running into lock-out issues and probably want to avoid drafting, say, Reinhardt and Blocker together since it'll leave you with no decent searchers for later games.

    Finally, if you're really struggling and just want that damned achievement, you can resort to save scumming. Simply close the game when the loss is clear (even when the loss message is up) and loading your save will put you back at the pick/ban of that game. Just ensure you alt-F4 before you get out of the match, because that's when the auto-save happens.
